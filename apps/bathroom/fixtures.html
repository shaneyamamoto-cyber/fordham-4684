TOILET — ONE-PIECE, LOW-PROFILE
1aCompact elongated
Short tank-to-bowl depth, rounded seat. Reads modern without being a full wall-hung install.
1bSkirted smooth-side
Skirted trapway hides all plumbing curves — a single unbroken surface, easiest to keep clean.
1cSquared architectural
Crisper edges throughout, less taper. Pairs with the squared vanity and mirror corners.
1qWall-hung, concealed tank
Bowl floats off the floor, tank hidden in the wall — just a small flush plate shows. Cleanest floor line of the set.
1rTwo-piece traditional
Separate tank bolted to the bowl, visible seam. The familiar everyday shape — easiest to source and service.
1sCorner-fit compact
Triangular tank back tucks into a corner. Only worth it if the layout needs the floor space back.
1tHigh-tank pull-chain
Tank mounted high on the wall, chain pull. A deliberate period-revival move, not a low-profile pick.
1uRound-bowl space saver
Round rather than elongated bowl — a few inches shorter front-to-back where clearance is tight.
MIRROR
1dPill / stadium
Fully rounded ends, slim copper frame. What's on the elevation now.
1eRounded rectangle
Softened corners only. Quieter, reads slightly more traditional over the vanity.
1fArched top
Full arch crown, flat bottom. The most vertical of the four — good if the basin feels wide.
1gRound
No corners at all. Reads smallest of the four at this basin width — best as a pair.
1vBacklit rectangle
Plain rectangle with a halo of light traced around the frame edge — the mirror itself is nearly invisible.
1wOctagon
Eight flat facets. More geometric than any of the other four — a deliberate accent piece, not a quiet backdrop.
1xRound pair
Two rounds at different sizes, one per basin — only makes sense if the basin ever becomes a double.
1yFrameless beveled
No visible frame at all — just a polished bevel catching light at the edge. Coolest, most minimal of the set.
TAP / FAUCET SET
1h3-piece wall, cross handles
Spout centred, two cross handles flanking. Locked on the current design.
1i2-hole, single lever
Spout plus a single lever escutcheon. Fewer holes, quieter wall — reads more minimal.
1jWall-mount lever pair
Long straight levers instead of crosses. Same 3-hole layout, more architectural, less ornate.
1aaSingle-lever monobloc
One body, one lever on top — a single hole in the wall does everything. The quietest possible tap.
1abBridge-style
A horizontal tube joins the two handles above the spout. Old-world plumbing-supply look.
1acWheel handles
Spoked wheel handles instead of crosses — reads more industrial-vintage than the locked set.
1adWaterfall spout
Flat slab spout, water sheets off the full width rather than a single stream. Most modern of the set.
1aeGooseneck high-arc
Tall single arch, single handle to the side. Clears a deep vessel basin better than the locked 3-piece.
1afGooseneck + single lever
The tall high-arc gooseneck spout of 1ae, but with a single straight lever mixer to the side instead of a cross handle — one lever sets flow and temperature. The cleanest high-clearance tap for a deep vessel basin.
SHOWERHEAD
1kCeiling rainhead
Ceiling-fed, wide flat head straight down. What's on the current design.
1lWall-arm rain
Wall-mounted gooseneck arm out to the same wide head — no ceiling penetration needed.
1mHandheld + slide bar
Vertical slide bar with a detachable handheld. Most flexible; adds a visible rail to the wall.
1afDual: rain + handheld
Fixed rain head plus a second handheld unit on its own bar. Two spray patterns without a diverter dance.
1agAdjustable angled arm
Ball-joint elbow lets the head tilt — useful where ceiling height or standing height varies.
1ahThermostatic panel
One vertical control tower runs rain, handheld and body jets off shared valving. Tidiest wall, priciest install.
1aiBody jets column
Three small jets stacked on the tub-side wall. Supplementary to a main head, not a replacement for one.
1ajSquare rain panel
Flat square head instead of the rounded rectangle — reads more architectural, less soft.
